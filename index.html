How Party Lines works

Predict the winner and the margin in 16 competitive 2026 Senate races. Your picks fill a map and line up on a number line from bluest to reddest, with the Senate balance they'd produce. Seal them before the deadline, then compare with everyone else and watch the real results get added to the line on election night.

How to play

  1. For each race, select the candidate you think wins and type the margin in points.
  2. Your picks save as you go, and you can change them as often as you like.
  3. When all 16 are filled in, seal them. Sealing is permanent: sealed picks can't be changed or unsealed.
  4. Picks close Tuesday, Nov 3, 2026 at 6:00 p.m. Eastern. Unsealed picks don't count.
  5. You can see another player's picks only once you've both sealed.

Scoring

  • Winners called: how many races you picked the right winner in. Ties go to whoever's margins were closer to the real results.
  • Average miss: how far your margins were from the real ones, in points, averaged across races. Lower is better.
  • Leaderboards: every sealed player is ranked on both metrics, overall and within your group. The top three get gold, silver and bronze, and tied players share a place.

How races are called

  • Winner: the AP race call, as reported by the New York Times.
  • Margin: the gap between the top two candidates at the moment a race reaches 95% of expected vote, or at Friday, Nov 6, 6:00 p.m. Central, whichever comes first.
  • Maine and Alaska use ranked-choice voting. They're scored on the final-round margin, after ranked-choice tabulation.

Additional notes
  • Montana is scored on the winner only. Its three-way race (Alani Bankhead is also running as a Democrat) makes the margin too volatile, so it counts toward winners called and the Senate balance but not average miss.
  • The Senate balance counts the 65 seats not up this year (Angus King and Bernie Sanders with the Democrats) and the other 19 races as safe for the party holding them.
  • Dan Osborn and Seth Bodnar count as independents.
  • A 50–50 Senate goes to the Republicans on the vice president's tiebreak.
